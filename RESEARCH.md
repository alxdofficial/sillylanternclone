# Research and architecture notes

Research checked 15 November 2026. This describes the design space, not a claim that every feature is in the prototype.

## How SillyTavern helps a writer

[SillyTavern](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern) is a locally run frontend for language-model backends, not a model itself. It is geared toward roleplay and interactive fiction, but its character and context tools are also useful for outlining and drafting fan fiction. Its many extensions and settings make it powerful; they also bring complexity a first-time writer may not need.

- **Character cards** describe a character's identity, personality, background, scenario, opening message, and examples of speech. A writer can make character voices consistent, or use a card as an AI writing partner. A chat belongs to a character, and a group can contain multiple characters. [Character docs](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/characters/).
- **Personas** define the identity and description of the writer's participant, with chat or character bindings. This can be an original character, a reader-insert, or simply a preferred point of view. [Persona docs](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern-Docs/blob/main/Usage/personas.md).
- **World Info / lorebooks** store many snippets about canon, relationships, places, or rules. Entries may activate on matching keywords or always be included; there are sophisticated controls for matching, recursive activation, insertion location and token budgets. This lets relevant canon enter context without putting an entire wiki in every prompt. [World Info docs](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/core-concepts/worldinfo/).
- **Chat editing and exploration** includes editable user and model messages, regenerating alternative responses (“swipes”), and branching/checkpoints. Authors can compare choices rather than accept the first answer. [Chatting docs](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/chatting/).
- **Prompt assembly and generation settings** combine character details, conversation, relevant world info, personas and instructions before calling a backend. Presets, sampling and context limits let experienced users tune style and behavior. These controls depend on the chosen backend.
- **Extensions** add summaries, retrieval from documents, search, image generation, voice and other modalities. Those may be valuable later, but add integration, cost and privacy decisions. [Extensions](https://docs.sillytavern.app/extensions/).

A story-focused harness should foreground scene intention, an editable manuscript, character and canon references, and choice among revisions. Chat roleplay and long-form manuscript editing overlap but are not identical workflows. A writer should remain able to inspect and edit the context, accept a suggestion deliberately, and export their work.

## Import and licensing

SillyTavern is [AGPL-3.0](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern). Reusing its source may be appropriate if fidelity to its extensive plugins, UI, chat formatting, lore selection and format compatibility becomes the priority; it also carries AGPL obligations that need review for the distribution or hosted deployment in question. Merely learning from publicly documented concepts does not require copying its code. A small independent implementation allows a narrower writing-first UX and a simpler integration, but is **not a SillyTavern clone** and does not inherit its mature ecosystem.

Compatibility is a concrete future feature, not a yes/no promise. Character Card V1 uses flat fields; V2 puts them in a `data` envelope and adds fields like examples, alternate greetings and `character_book`. Cards are often PNGs with base64 JSON in a `chara` metadata chunk; V3 also uses `ccv3`. The [V2 card specification](https://github.com/malfoyslastname/character-card-spec-v2/blob/main/spec_v2.md) calls out semantics, including lorebook activation, alternate greetings, metadata that must stay out of prompts, and preservation of unknown extensions. A real importer should retain the original data, warn about unsupported fields, bound file and decompression sizes, treat imported instructions as untrusted, and test real files. Importing a name and description alone is not full compatibility. Worldbook, chat and persona imports are separate formats and need their own tests.

## Model access

**Codex.** The official CLI supports [non-interactive `codex exec`](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/non-interactive-mode), and by default it reuses its saved sign-in. The [authentication documentation](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/auth) distinguishes ChatGPT subscription access from usage-billed API-key access; check `codex login status` rather than assume which is active. Usage is subject to the account's limits and service rules, and a writing model can still decline or alter material under provider policies. Officially supported [Codex app-server](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/app-server) is a richer integration for asynchronous events, streaming, cancellation, approvals and session lifecycle; OpenAI recommends [Sign in with ChatGPT](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/token-sharing-open-source/codex-app-server) for apps that want users to see and control their account access. For an initial private local prototype, the CLI is a lower-complexity bridge. A product intended for other users should investigate the current app-server sign-in requirements and avoid copying a person's local auth cache.

**Claude.** Anthropic's [help article](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15036540-use-the-claude-agent-sdk-with-your-claude-plan) says that signed-in `claude -p`, Agent SDK and third-party app usage currently draw from subscription limits; it also discusses plan-linked API credits for some tiers. Its [legal and compliance page](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance) says developers must not offer Claude.ai login in their own applications or route requests through plan credentials on behalf of users, while distinguishing the hosting of an unmodified Claude Code binary with end users authenticating directly. These statements are not straightforward to reconcile for every product design. Do not embed login, handle tokens or promise that a custom bridge is permitted without resolving the specific use case with Anthropic and rechecking current terms. Running Claude Code directly as an end user is a separate flow. This prototype should not imply Claude is supported unless actually implemented and verified.

## Is MCP the right tool?

[MCP](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/learn/architecture) connects an AI host to tools and resources; it is not a model, subscription, authentication bypass, or substitute for a generation engine. A story MCP server could expose narrow operations like `get_story_bible`, `search_lore`, `read_scene`, or `propose_revision` to either CLI. Read-only tools and explicit approval before any write are sensible defaults. It becomes valuable when multiple agents or clients need the same story library, dynamic retrieval, or structured actions. For one local application with a small selected story and a deterministic context builder, sending the chosen context directly is simpler to inspect and test; add MCP only when its interoperability provides real value.

## Operational limits and next steps

- A coding agent is not a dedicated prose API: response time, token usage, style, refusal behavior and context limits may differ. Test with Jess's real use cases.
- A read-only CLI sandbox does not mean the model is unable to read local files. Prompt instructions are not a security boundary; isolate the process, narrow tools/permissions, avoid untrusted public access and don't run arbitrary imported scripts. Story text is sent to the provider in generation requests.
- Prioritize export/backup, revisions, selectable context, clear generation errors, and easy onboarding. Then evaluate selective lore, alternatives, branches, character-card interoperability and a formal MCP server based on what Jess actually uses.

## OpenRouter as an optional, separately configured backend

[OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/support/) supplies an OpenAI-compatible chat-completions endpoint and routes requests to model providers. It does **not** use a ChatGPT or Claude subscription: it requires its own API key. Paid models consume OpenRouter credits, at model- and provider-dependent rates. Some [free model variants](https://openrouter.ai/collections/free-models/) have $0 model pricing without a credit purchase but still require an OpenRouter account/key, are rate-limited, and may change or be unavailable. Thus it is an optional alternative, not a way to use a coding subscription.

Users can choose a particular model; the service then selects a provider endpoint or, when enabled, falls back. [Routing controls](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/how-we-rebuilt-provider-routing/) include provider order, allowlists, sorting and fallback behavior. Provider defaults and availability can change; do not assume the same endpoint or model is used every time. In particular, do not silently switch models or providers when the user cares about style, pricing, or who receives their work. Let the writer choose and make the resulting errors understandable.

Model capability, context length, moderation and willingness to generate particular fictional content vary by model and provider; nothing about the gateway makes any specific kind of content universally available. The provider's terms and OpenRouter's own policies still apply. Test writing quality and acceptable behavior with representative prompts rather than promise that a specific model is unrestricted.

[OpenRouter's privacy policy](https://openrouter.ai/privacy/) says inputs are transmitted to the selected or automatically routed model provider, and providers' retention and training policies differ. [Zero-data-retention guidance](https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/zero-data-retention/) explains that `provider.zdr: true` filters for eligible inference endpoints; `provider.data_collection: "deny"` is another routing control. ZDR does not mean local-only processing, remove metadata, cover the app's own logs or third-party tools, or automatically apply unless enforced. OpenRouter describes exceptions for opt-in logging and CSAM review. Confirm actual plan availability and endpoint eligibility instead of presenting these controls as universal guarantees.

**Local Claude check, 15 November 2026:** `claude auth status` reported `loggedIn: false`, `authMethod: none`, `apiProvider: firstParty`. No login, configuration change, or generation request was made, so Claude subscription generation is unverified and no API inference was knowingly billed by this check.

## Upstream implementation inspection and parity priorities

On 15 November 2026, shallow-cloned the official SillyTavern **release** branch into a temporary directory and inspected commit [`06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127`](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/tree/06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127). These links pin the reviewed code. No code was copied into Story Studio.

- [Main generation assembly](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/blob/06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127/public/script.js#L4618) scans the conversation in reverse order for World Info, makes character/persona/scenario available as global scan data, inserts world-info examples and depth injections, and adds the persona description. [Chat-completion assembly](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/blob/06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127/public/scripts/openai.js#L1367) builds prompt items and uses a configurable prompt manager; it is more than concatenating the latest draft and lore.
- [World Info selection](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/blob/06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127/public/scripts/world-info.js#L4709) sets a budget proportional to context with an optional cap; processes sorted entries with configurable recursion and timed effects; and tracks which entries activated. World info has multiple insertion destinations and activation features. A simple string matcher is only a subset.
- [Swipe state](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/blob/06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127/public/script.js#L6833) stores multiple response strings, an active `swipe_id`, and separate metadata per alternative. Editing and selecting alternatives operate on that state; this is not merely overwriting the last answer.
- [Card parser](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/blob/06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127/src/character-card-parser.js) and [character import/export](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/blob/06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127/src/endpoints/characters.js#L181) handle more than flat JSON, including PNG metadata and character-book association. Its [V2/V3 validator](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/blob/06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127/src/validator/TavernCardValidator.js) is useful reference for fixture design.
- [OpenRouter settings](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/blob/06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127/public/scripts/openai.js#L2889) capture provider, model, fallbacks and related routing options; the [server adapter](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/blob/06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127/src/endpoints/openai.js#L145) targets OpenRouter's chat-completions endpoint.

Recommended order for closing the practical gap: (1) reliable persisted multi-message chat, editable messages, true swipe alternatives and branches; (2) character/persona/scenario fields, opening and examples with inspectable assembled context and budget; (3) import/export of common JSON/PNG cards with faithful field preservation and explicit unsupported-field warnings; (4) deterministic World Info matching and budgets with fixtures, then add selective/recursive/depth semantics deliberately; (5) provider/model choice and transparent cost/privacy handling; (6) group chat, extension compatibility, elaborate prompt management and other modalities based on demand. Claim only the semantics actually tested. If broad plugin/UI compatibility is the real goal, reconsider a fork of the AGPL project rather than pretending an independent subset has parity.

## Implemented in the revised browser build

The layout was checked against the upstream chat screen and source CSS at the pinned revision. Story Studio follows the dark, centered scrolling transcript, persistent bottom composer, top tool strip, side drawers and per-turn actions. It uses labeled controls and an explicit provider picker. It does not reproduce SillyTavern's themes, backgrounds, extensions or complete navigation.

The shared builder in `public/prompt.js` follows the block order in the upstream [default Chat Completion preset](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/blob/06bde939fb1e9c4c8d8641d810f0a916b5bce127/default/content/presets/openai/Default.json), using original writing instructions. Lore can precede character definitions or activate later in the prompt; the dynamic subset checks literal keys against recent turns and the current direction. Character and persona macros expand in the settings blocks. Examples precede role-tagged history, an author's note is positioned before up to four recent turns, and post-history instructions come after history. OpenRouter receives the role-tagged messages. Codex and manual handoff use their text rendering. Prompt preview uses the same builder as generation. Caps are character-based; upstream token accounting, customizable order, example-message parsing, recursion, and its full lorebook rules still differ.

## Character, persona and group-chat comparison

SillyTavern stores reusable character cards and lets the writer combine them into groups. Group members share history. The default Swap mode inserts the active member's card for that reply. Upstream also supports joined cards, muted members, and Natural, List, Pooled and Manual reply ordering. Its lorebooks can attach to a character, persona, chat, or global settings. See [SillyTavern groups](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/core-concepts/groupchats/) and [World Info](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/core-concepts/worldinfo/).

Story Studio now implements a story-local cast and explicit speaker selection. It retains the original speaker on swipes, preserves named turns in shared history, and uses the selected card for description, personality, scenario, and examples. The writer's persona and lore remain shared story fields. It does not attempt automatic reply order, joined cards, reusable global card profiles or scoped lorebook bindings.
