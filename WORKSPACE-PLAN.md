# Story Studio workspace and writing tools

The manuscript stays in view while the author writes, reviews a finding, or follows a reference. The left dock is for navigation, the right dock is for writing tools, and the bottom docks are for composing and Story Flow. See the [current controls](README.md#review-a-draft) and [implementation checks](TESTING.md#resizable-workspace-and-writing-tools).

## Workspace layout

```text
Writing                 Chapters  |   Manuscript   |  Writing tools
Reviewing a tool                    Manuscript   ⇄   Themes
Narrow screen           Manuscript   ↔   tool view with a return control
Bottom edge             Story Flow dock  /  Composer dock
```

**Writing.** Open a compact **Writing tools** list on the right where it fits. Start with **In this scene**, **Pacing**, **Themes**, **Setups & payoffs**, and **Continuity**. Give each one a short preview made from the current scene or chapter. Keep **Chapters** and **Writing tools** available as labeled workspace controls even when their docks are closed. Preserve the readable prose width as the dock widths change.

**Using a tool.** Open a roughly 50/50 manuscript and tool split when both panes meet their usable width. Temporarily tuck away the left outline and keep a **Chapters** control to open it on demand. **Back to tools** restores the list and previous layout. **Close writing tools** restores the last writing layout. Keep the current scene, draft, tool scope, and both scroll positions while the author moves between these states. When a citation is clicked, scroll to it while keeping the tool and its results visible. A citation in another story can open a small read-only excerpt, with an explicit action to open that story.

**Compact windows.** Make the outline a drawer and give detailed tools the full width. **Back to manuscript** reveals the cited scene. **Back to [tool]** returns to the same finding. Keep at most one overlay open. Use actual workspace width and height for these decisions so embedded windows, zoom, and side-by-side browser use get the same behavior. Test a 50/50 split around a 1,000 px workspace and adjust from the minimum usable width of each pane.

**Resize and restore.** On desktop, drag a side dock’s inner edge to set width or a bottom dock’s top edge to set height. Use a named, focusable divider with arrow controls and give the same options through click or tap presets and **Reset layout**. On touch, start with compact and expanded heights. Remember the last useful size and the author’s explicit open state in browser preferences. Keep temporary layout decisions, such as hiding Chapters for a tool or collapsing Story Flow when the keyboard opens, out of those preferences. Prefer a short slide from the dock edge on open and close, immediate updates during resizing, and an instant state change with reduced motion. [VS Code custom layout](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/configure/custom-layout) · [Scrivener split editor](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/see-more-of-your-project-splitting-the-scrivener-editor) · [Photoshop collapsed panels](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/desktop/get-started/learn-the-basics/collapse-expand-icons.html) · [W3C splitter pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/windowsplitter/) · [W3C alternatives to dragging](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements)

## Composer, Story Flow, and long fields

- **Composer.** Keep mode and model choices in the header, with the collapse control and **Expand editor** beside them. Keep the context meter and Sensitive scene control in the footer, with **Send** at the right edge. **Collapse composer** drops it to a **Show composer** button at the bottom. In a short window, move model details into an accessible menu and keep the input and Send visible. Resizing changes the typing area within the dock. The text scrolls internally after the dock reaches its limit.
- **Story Flow.** Collapse independently to **Story Flow** or **Story Flow · 3 ideas**, depending on state. If it is empty, **Suggest directions** starts the request. If it has choices, **New directions** replaces them. Collapsing, reopening, and previewing retain the current choices and running request. Display the three directions in one row where space permits. Use a single-row card strip with visible next and previous controls on compact screens. Increasing the dock height can reveal more of a preview. In short landscape, the composer takes precedence and Story Flow can peek from the bottom row. A new suggestion should never cover the prose the author is reading.
- **Expand editor.** Give the composer, manual prose, cast descriptions, World, Writing notes, and long setup and import fields a consistent **Expand editor** control beside the field label. Open the same draft in a spacious, wrapped plain-text editor with a fixed header and a single scroll area. On phones, make it full-screen. Within a setup dialog, expand the current field in place so the form stays at the same step. **Done** returns the text to its original form as a draft. A deliberate **Discard expanded edits** restores the text from before opening. Closing with Escape retains the draft. Keep caret position, selection, scroll, and undo behavior testable across the transition. Stay with the original field’s Save or Send action so expanding never submits text. [W3C dialog pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/)

## What each writing tool shows on open

Use a preview that tells the author what they can do next. Start from the selected scene or chapter. Once the author chooses a review scope, keep it stable while browsing evidence.

| Tool | Visible by default | Typical action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **In this scene** | Scene time, mentioned cast, and relevant setting or writing notes. | Open the cast member or related graph event. |
| **Pacing** | Scene length, estimated quoted dialogue, character mentions, and recorded plot or relationship changes. Use a stable comparison scale across chapters. | Select a point to see which passage or event contributed to it. |
| **Themes** | Pinned themes first, then concrete recurring phrases or details with the number of scenes they appear in. | Select prose to pin it. Find exact wording locally or ask AI for related ideas. Keep all accepted citations for a theme. |
| **Setups & payoffs** | Tracked threads grouped as **Open**, **Paid off**, or **Set aside**, with AI suggestions underneath. | Select a passage to track a setup. Click **Link payoff** and choose the passage in the manuscript. A suggested match shows why the two passages are related. |
| **Continuity** | Results of local timeline and graph checks plus any still-current reviewed findings. | Compare the passages, mark an intentional difference, or dismiss. Keep other-story comparison and canon notes available in the detail view. |

For example, *The Late Ferry* can display **Filed tooth on the brass key · setup in Scene 2 · payoff in Scene 3**, with both passages available from the same row. Its unanswered beacon flash belongs in **Open**. A continuity candidate can pair Lina’s first handling of the log with Mae’s later use of the key and ask whether the cabinet was already open. Pacing copy should describe what changed in the text or graph so the author can decide whether a quiet scene serves the story. [ProWritingAid pacing check](https://help.prowritingaid.com/article/47-how-to-use-the-pacing-check) · [Word Editor’s linked findings](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/word/check-grammar-spelling-and-more-in-word-for-the-web)

## Automatic signals and highlights

1. **Start with inexpensive local data.** Recalculate after a save or a short idle period, only for changed scenes and affected neighbors. Show narrative shape from words, quoted dialogue, paragraph lengths, named cast references, and recorded developments. For comparisons, try **recorded changes per 100 words** alongside the raw count. If the graph is sparse, use **recorded** in the label. Repeated distinctive phrases can suggest motifs with exact evidence.
2. **Trial embeddings for echoes and topic shifts.** Compare paragraphs or sentence groups with earlier passages in the same story and with their immediate neighbors. Similarity can surface an earlier echo. A jump in distance can suggest a change in focus. Compare both to a simple local word-overlap baseline and to the manuscript’s own patterns. Show the matched passage or the boundary and let the author judge. Token count estimates reading volume and model cost. Embedding distance estimates semantic similarity. Publish a distinct-development count only when each development can be traced to a passage or a saved event. [Sentence-BERT](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1410/) · [TextTiling](https://aclanthology.org/J97-1003/)
3. **Use similarity to find possible conflicts.** Compare claims about the same character, object, location, or time. Directly flag verifiable conflicts in structured facts. Use a narrow inference pass for ambiguous prose and require two excerpts with relevant dates or speakers. A change of state, a flashback, a rumor, and a different viewpoint are useful contrast cases. Show **Check these passages** with the evidence; skip a global contradiction or prose-quality score. [SummaC’s treatment of evidence granularity](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.10/)
4. **Highlight on demand.** Clicking a finding reveals its passage automatically. An optional **Highlight in text** toggle marks all results from the active tool, with a brief legend and next and previous controls. Use labels and text links alongside color. Clear the overlay on tool close or source changes. Use stable scene IDs, fingerprints, and canonical paragraph anchors. Start with paragraph markers until aliases and typographic transformations can be mapped reliably to precise ranges. Keep highlights out of manuscript text and exports.

The right panel can open immediately with local calculations and still-current cached results. Fresh remote analysis uses one visible action such as **Find themes with AI**, with the model and scope shown there. Opening or resizing a dock, selecting evidence, and linking a payoff make zero model calls. If the local embedding trial is useful, run it in a background worker, start with bounded exact comparisons, and cache by story, text span, eligibility, and model version. Recompute ordering-dependent results when the author edits, reshuffles, or branches. Keep existing context rules on every provider request. [ONNX Runtime Web worker guidance](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/tutorials/web/performance-diagnosis.html)

## Implementation order and acceptance checks

1. **Workspace first.** Prototype wide, compact, and short layouts with the existing demo. Then implement one layout state for the four docks, the shared draft editor, resize controls, and focus restoration. Test draft survival, keyboard sizing, touch presets, Send visibility, Story Flow preview and insertion, and restoration after switching stories or crossing a breakpoint.
2. **Move the current tools.** Reuse the existing Revision logic and passage references. Migrate old Revision links into the right pane before removing the standalone tab. Link Graph continuity to the same review surface and retain the unsaved Graph edit check. Keep chapter navigation in one place. Have first-time readers find and follow the key’s payoff without a walkthrough.
3. **Make previews automatic.** Add local summaries and select-to-highlight, cache derived data from the relevant scenes, and keep existing saved pins and threads usable after migration. Verify with no API key, excluded text, edited passages, and drafts or requests in progress. Show **Passage changed** if the original quote can no longer be found uniquely.
4. **Evaluate embeddings before exposing a metric.** Compare local word matching and embeddings on deliberate echoes, paraphrases, changed possession, conflicting dates, rumors, flashbacks, and multilingual text. Measure which of the first few suggestions an author would use, and whether cold and warm analyses stay out of the editor’s way. Ship the signals that make relevant passages easier to find. [CheckList behavioral testing](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.442/)

Run the interaction checks at 320 and 390 px, in short landscape, across the 820–1,120 px range, and at wide desktop. Include 200% browser zoom, reduced motion, keyboard-only operation, and a physical phone with its keyboard open.

## Writing tools as a first-open review

The tool should answer one writing question as soon as it opens. Show up to three useful observations first, each with a scene link. Keep the full list and filter controls below. Scope changes should filter evidence already available. The manuscript should remain beside the finding on wide screens, with a return link to the same finding on phones.

| Tool | Question it should answer | First-open result | Action worth taking |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **In this scene** | What should I keep in mind here? | Scene time and viewpoint if established, present cast, and the specific graph or writing note that applies. | Open the cast profile or dated graph event. |
| **Pacing** | Where does the story change speed or spend unusually long on one beat? | A chapter strip with a small number of comparisons, such as “Scene 4 is twice as long as its neighbors and records no new plot events.” Also show how much dialogue there is. | Open the scene next to its neighbors. Use a short AI explanation of what the scene is doing when one is available. A long or quiet scene can be a deliberate breather. |
| **Themes** | Which ideas or images develop across the book? | Pinned themes and evidence-backed patterns. For example, “Who gets to correct the record?” can link Lina preserving the original entry and Mae signing a correction. Patterns should show how the idea changes across scenes. | Compare the passages, pin a theme, or search for an exact phrase. Keep exact word matches labeled as such. |
| **Setups & payoffs** | What did I lead readers to expect, and where did I answer it? | Open threads with the earliest setup and latest relevant mention. Then show resolved pairs, such as the filed tooth on the key and the scene where the key fits the cabinet. Unconfirmed pairings are suggestions. | Jump to the original setup, link a payoff, or set a thread aside. The reader can see tracked threads and suggestions separately. |
| **Continuity** | Do chronology, facts, or character knowledge appear to conflict? | The most actionable unchecked finding, two quotes, and the specific fact in tension. If nothing surfaced, show how much was checked and whether it was the graph, manuscript, or both. | Compare both scenes, mark an intentional exception, or dismiss. Keep viewpoint, rumor, flashbacks, and ordinary changes of state in the evidence. |

### Manual review and its limits

- Pacing, exact phrase matches, pinned themes, tracked threads, and graph checks are computed locally. Pacing measures length, quoted dialogue, cast mentions, and dated events. The background pass adds evidence-backed observations about the scene’s narrative function.
- Themes, thread suggestions, and prose continuity have a separate, manual review route. It packages eligible prose for the requested scope, canon, and optional comparison prose as one tool-using request. It caps the input at 24,000 estimated tokens or the provider budget if smaller, reserves 4,096 output tokens, and accepts up to 10 findings. Long scope requests fail at that boundary even if the selected model could handle more.
- Evidence must match exact, unique text in the supplied passage. A library revision comparison can reject a result when an unrelated story changes. The server reuses identical successful requests while running. The browser can restore matching results after a reload in the same tab. A fresh session starts with local data and saved notes.

### Background review workflow

1. **Build a scene index from the committed story.** Store stable scene and paragraph IDs, canonical offsets, chapter and story dates, eligibility, and relevant structured metadata. Calculate pacing and structural checks on save. Present those immediately even on a fresh import. Cached AI findings are optional enrichment.
2. **Extract observations in batches.** Pass stable instructions and dated scene excerpts to the existing provider-neutral agent harness. Prefer whole scenes and split an oversized scene at paragraph boundaries. Carry source IDs, ordering, the necessary nearby text, and a bounded slate of relevant cast, graph, or writing notes. Budget per call from the chosen model’s context window. Reserve tools and output, leave headroom for estimation, and retry a context rejection by splitting the batch. A large book makes more calls without requiring a larger model window.
3. **Link evidence across batches and chapters.** Index the extracted entities, dated claims, unresolved questions, and candidate themes. Compare potentially related items across the whole book, including later payoffs for early setups. Fetch the exact two passages for a confirmation pass. Group recurring ideas and rank what the author should inspect first. Use structured facts and phrase retrieval initially. Treat an embedding score as a retrieval hint if it improves recall on representative books.
4. **Publish a durable, scoped result.** Store analysis alongside local application data, keyed by story source hashes, canon dependencies, model, and analyzer version. Never merge suggestions into notes or graph canon without the author’s action. Invalidate modified scenes and dependent findings. Keep still-valid evidence visible while a replacement batch is running. Recheck the affected sources before publishing, so an unrelated save leaves the job intact. Re-evaluate chronology-dependent findings after scene moves, chapter changes, or rewinds.
5. **Precompute by default per story.** Local checks are always available. The first time a story opens with a configured review model, the browser enrolls it. The server works in the background with one batch at a time, giving interactive requests first access between batches. It saves checkpoints and observes edits through the saved library. The tools show reviewed and held scene counts, the review model, an estimated daily budget, and a pause control. Manual review stays useful for an ad hoc theme query or comparison story.

### Existing stories and future updates

The story library stays the source of truth. Existing data uses the same scene and eligibility rules as a new manuscript. Open a story once to enroll it. A first scan fills in eligible passages that have no checkpoints. Pausing preserves completed work. Resume reviews new or changed passages and any unchecked links. The review worker never writes chapters, accepted pins, author notes, or graph state.

Background observations and pairing decisions have independent recipe versions in `revision-background-review.mjs`. Bump the extraction version if passage splitting, observation prompts, or observation semantics change. Bump the link version if topic grouping, pairing, or link validation changes. A link-only change keeps passage extraction and refreshes links. Source hashes include the selected take, in-story timestamp, and exact ranges. Matching pacing and setup observations can stay visible during a refresh. Ineligible, edited, or removed sources drop out of published results. A storage-format change can discard and rebuild derived cache entries while carrying forward the saved model and pause preference.

### Which in-text signals are useful

Repetition marks a specific later occurrence that the writer can read aloud and decide to keep. The local scan uses exact nearby phrases with distinctive content words and a 900-word lookback. Density combines recently unseen words with concrete anchors such as cast names or numbers. It compares each paragraph against the selected scope so ordinary vocabulary does not shade every paragraph. In scopes with several paragraphs, the default selects about the top tenth at most and the optional subtler layer expands coverage up to about a quarter. Treat both overlays as prompts to reread.

A sentence-rhythm overlay could point to clusters of similarly long sentences using local sentence segmentation. A quoted-dialogue overlay could show where a run of dialogue starts and ends. Semantic echoes would need a separate signal. Embed passages once per text fingerprint, then use cosine similarity to retrieve earlier passages and show them together. Evaluate it on exact repeats, paraphrases, and unrelated passages that share a setting or mood before applying it across a manuscript.

### Prompts and validation

Use a shared review instruction for source handling, cited evidence, and the result format. Add a short task for the requested finding. **Pacing** explains the function of a measured transition or contrast. **Themes** describes a repeated idea with evidence from distinct scenes and explains how it develops. **Threads** extracts a specific promise and cites a later scene only when it follows through. **Continuity** identifies the exact facts in tension and accounts for viewpoint, uncertainty, and in-story time. Model outputs are candidates. The app resolves passage IDs, checks quotes and scope, and keeps original and display names separate.

Send only prose allowed by the existing general AI-context rules. Run local checks on the local manuscript and verify eligibility again when fetching evidence for a provider. Test a manuscript longer than any per-call budget with a payoff in the final chapter, a long single scene, a partial provider failure, and edits made while review is running. Track reviewed scene coverage, verified citation rate, useful findings in the first screen, and cost per completed chapter. Chunking removes the one-request ceiling. Provider budgets, rate limits, and the need to show partial coverage still apply.
